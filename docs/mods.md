<h3 dir="ltr">Design Tip&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Eras add narrative weight to each phase of the game. You can create campaign arcs or story-driven shifts by preselecting a sequence of Eras.</p>

| Glitches Deck  | <h3 dir="ltr">Overview&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">The Glitches Deck introduces unpredictable, often disruptive effects into the game. Glitches are short-term anomalies that mimic magical errors or environmental chaos. They can shift strategies instantly and create new tactical challenges for all players.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Setup&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Shuffle the Glitches Deck and place it face-down in the center of the table. Decide whether you will use time-based triggers, dice rolls, or in-game events to activate Glitches.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Rotation Timing&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">A new Glitch is revealed every 1-3 full turn rotations. When a new Glitch enters play, the previous one is typically discarded. Some groups choose to keep two Glitches active simultaneously for added complexity.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Trigger-Based Mode&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Reveal a new Glitch when specific in-game actions occur. Examples include: when a creature dies, a player loses 5 or more life in a turn, or when a new Plane is revealed. This mode keeps Glitches tied closely to in-game drama. (Agree condtions in advnace)</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Dice Roll Mode&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">At the start of each player's turn, roll a six-sided die. On a result of 5 or 6, reveal a new Glitch. This mode introduces a random but fair chance for disruption.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Glitch Duration&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Most Glitches last until the start of the next turn or until replaced. You may mark their duration with a counter, token, or written tracker. Some Glitches may have permanent effects, but this should be indicated clearly on the card.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Stacking Glitches&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">By default, only one Glitch may be active at a time. If playing with stacking, limit to two simultaneous Glitches. Be cautious - this mode creates intense chaos and can unbalance more structured decks.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Glitch Effects and Resolution&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Glitches affect all players equally unless otherwise specified. Some modify core rules (such as draw steps, casting restrictions, or mana generation). Players must resolve effects in order if multiple Glitches trigger at once. (newest first)</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Design Tip&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Use Glitches to challenge players, level power dynamics, or introduce moments of pure madness. For more control, let the lowest life player decide when a new Glitch is drawn.</p>

| Quests Deck    | <h3 dir="ltr">Overview&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">The Quest Deck introduces optional side-objectives that players may complete for powerful rewards. These cards add layers of strategy, risk, and excitement.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Setup&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Each quest describes a specific condition and a reward for fulfilling it. Players can only complete quests they have drawn or those revealed globally.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Start-of-Game Draft (Standard Mode)&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Each player draws or chooses one Quest at the start of the game. Players may optionally mulligan once to draw a different Quest. Once completed, the Quest card is discarded.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Milestone Reveal (Slow Burn)&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Every three full turn rotations, reveal a Quest to the center of the play area. The first player to complete it claims the reward and discards it.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Quest Cascade (Power Mode)&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Players draw a new Quest every turn or immediately after completing one.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Event Trigger (Chaos Style)&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Reveal or draw a Quest when a Glitch ends or a new Plane is entered. This mode encourages unpredictable quest injections into gameplay.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Shop Mechanic (Economy Style)&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Players may 'buy' a new Quest by paying 12 life or sacrificing 7 Treasure tokens.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Additional Rules&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Players may not complete more than one Quest per turn unless otherwise stated. Quest rewards resolve immediately unless otherwise noted. Quests are personal unless explicitly defined as global.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Design Tip&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Tailor quest frequency to your group's pace and deck power levels. For faster formats, use fewer quests. For campaign or RPG-style sessions, increase quest interaction and complexity.</p>

| Roles Deck     | <h3 dir="ltr">Overview&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">The Roles Deck assigns each player a unique identity or passive power that alters how they approach the game. You can choose to have roles be public and known from the start, or kept secret and revealed only when specific conditions are met.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Setup&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Each player draws one Role card at the beginning of the game. Decide as a group whether to play with public or secret roles. Secret roles are revealed only when their condition is triggered or by player choice.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Public Roles&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">These provide passive abilities or rule-bending powers that are always in effect. Examples include reduced casting costs, life gain triggers, or enhanced combat abilities.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Secret Roles&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Secret roles offer hidden objectives. If their unique win condition is met, the player wins-even if the standard win conditions aren't fulfilled. Secret roles may not interact directly with opponents until revealed.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Rules for Secret Roles&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Players may not bluff about their role unless the group agrees in advance. A secret role is only revealed when its condition is successfully met or if the player voluntarily discloses it. Only one secret win can be claimed per game. If multiple players reveal secrets at once, the one who met the condition first wins.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Role Interaction&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Role effects stack with other card abilities. Some cards may reference or interfere with role status (e.g., forcing a reveal).&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Multiplayer Tips&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Secret roles are most fun in games with 4+ players. Consider adding a neutral or antagonist role with disruptive goals for added complexity.&nbsp;</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Design Tip&nbsp;</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Roles are a great way to balance power levels across playgroups or encourage new strategies. Combine with Quests for maximum flavor.&nbsp;</p>
